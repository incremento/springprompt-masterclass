Chart gallery

Thirteen chart types, one per slideEach headline states the finding; the chart is there to prove it. All the numbers are made up.

One number, said once, is often the whole slide

stat A single figure with a sentence under it beats a chart with one bar.

The bakery had its best week of the year

stat Two to four tiles. A delta says which way it moved; good says whether that is welcome.

Twenty-six in every hundred commuters now cycle

dots A share people can count. Good for “1 in 4”; no good past a few hundred units.

Sourdough outsells the next loaf two to one

hbar Ranked bars, sorted for you, value at the tip. highlight picks the bar that matters.

Saturday takes as much as two weekdays

bar Vertical bars for a handful of categories with a natural order, such as days.

A £4.80 loaf earns only 10p more than a £3.40 coffee

segmented One whole, split into at most four coloured parts. Same part, same colour, in both bars.

Flat white, £3.40

Sourdough loaf, £4.80

Half of all commutes take under half an hour

histogram How one measure is spread. Give raw values or ready-made bins.

A standard loaf costs 40% more than it did in 2019

line One series needs no legend. An annotation marks the one point worth talking about.

Cycling passed both the bus and walking in 2023

line Up to four series, each named at its end. More than four: highlight one and let the rest go grey.

Every way to work got slower, except the bike

slope Two moments, one line per item. Use it when the direction of each line is the story.

Teenagers claw back more than two hours of sleep at the weekend

dumbbell The gap between two values, row by row. Hollow dot is the first value, solid the second.

Oat milk went from last to first in three years

rank Position over time, when the order matters more than the amounts.

Nine years from a market stall to a second shop

timeline Dated events on one axis, spaced by real time. Five or six events is plenty.

The longer the commute, the shorter the night

scatter Two measures per dot. A trend line and one shaded region say where to look.

Every two kilos you shave off a bike doubles the price

scatter A log axis for prices, a frontier for the best buys, names on the highlighted few.

Avocado toast earns the most per plate and barely sells

scatter quadrants turn two measures into four decisions.

Weekdays peak at 8am; weekends peak three hours later

heatmap Two categories and one number: darker is more. highlight outlines a cell.

Guess first: which month sells the most umbrellas?

June. Summer showers catch people without one.
